# Gaius Octavian Marcinus

The sweltering sun pierced through the uniform blanket of blue that draped over the sky. The air in Malpais spread itself thin, and the soft scent of desert foliage peppered each breath. 

<!--more-->

Gaius stepped out of the military office and onto the packed dirt thoroughfare filled with hundreds of civilians all going about their business.

He was taller than most and muscular too, with a healthy frame that followed him up to his square chin and brown hair. He always wore a sour face, and his brown eyes were in a permanent squint. His face was shaven since it grew in patchy against his light tan skin. His chest heaved as he took a fresh breath of air.

The Malpais forum rose before him on the other side of the street—a giant mass of vendors, soldiers, slaves, prostitutes, and gods knew what other degeneracy. He even heard the laughter of children and the squealing of livestock and sacrifices.

The city used to be called Grants before Mars remade the world, but the long stretches of lava rock stretching several miles south and unarable land had let a new name take over. There were many Malpaises Gaius knew, but this was his favorite. He'd grown attached in the few months he'd been stationed there: good people and little to complain about. The town had grown fast and now teetered between vibrant and cramped.

He was happy to get away from praetorians and their arrogant attitudes. He could always complain about them, but the enforcement in every town was filled with self-important blowhards. They couldn't have glory, so they replaced it with sadistic posturing. Fumes started to rise, and he decided it was best he kept them away from his thoughts. Besides, he was now out onto the streets, the actual lifeblood of Legion life. The sunlight toasted his skin while he searched the scene.

On the far side of the forum sat a local *thermopolium* specializing in chili and soups. He made his way through the crowds and finally spotted his friends Asinus and Fredericus already chowing down.

He cracked one of his rare smiles. “Heus, leave some for the rest of us.” They were in the same unit; Asinus was short with a large nose, while Fredericus was much taller and sported a flatter one.

Asinus looked up stupidly, his mouth bulging with chili-noodles con iguana. He beamed back with surprise and joy. He gulped the rest of it down and smiled. “Gai! How was your briefing? We had just started wondering if you had died, or worse, been forced to guard slaves.”

Gaius placed his hand on Asinus' shoulder then let out a massive sigh. “That at least would leave me with some honor; instead, I have been assigned as an escort to Senator Balbus while he travels to Flagstaff. We're leaving today or tomorrow, and it'll take weeks. I'll be sent to the dam directly from there.”

Fredericus stopped eating. “What a waste, why does he need an escort just to get to Flagstaff? Doesn't he have slaves?” He then started chugging down the last bit of water from his canteen.

“Well, he's a Senator, so he should. I don't understand why I've been given this assignment either, but I will carry it out nonetheless.” Gaius lowered himself onto the stone bench, sitting for the first time that day.

Asinus let out a half-chuckle and a crooked smile. “Truly a son of Caesar you are. Make sure to dutifully wipe his backside and play the wife as well.”

“Tace, Asine. One day I'll dine with Caesar in his tent, and you'll dine on _cunnus culiboniarum_.”

“I doubt you'd be so resigned to your fate if you'd ever met Balbus. He's the least Roman-like man I've ever seen!” Asinus dismissed him with a waved hand. “You'll see. Just make sure you meet us in Vegas. Mars is not as forgiving of insults as I.”

“I will placate the gods with a sacrifice after I leave here. I do not dare take on duty without acquiring their blessing,” he glanced toward the temple rising up from the side of the forum.

“Did you hear those debauched NCR cowards put up a memorial to honor themselves? With Mars, we might get justice for what they did to Demetrius,” Fredericus passionately informed the others, hatred and indignity in every word. He hated the NCR more than anyone, but Gaius still snapped, “Of course I know that!” His voice was tinged with emotion. The act still stung when he thought of it. The tender thoughts of Demetrius flashed across his mind—his little engineer. He almost felt the soft brown, messy hair and smelled the slight musk of oil. For a second, he could swear his boy was with him, but no, he wasn't. “I don't need reminding. I'll be in the Mojave. They'll pay.”

They spent a few more minutes there chatting and joking, taking in the balmy desert breeze. Eventually, Gaius bid the two _valete_ and then continued a few buildings down to the large Temple of Mars that took up an entire edge of the forum.

---

The steps were made of flaking metal and rust plastered with earth, and so were the columns that guarded the stoa leading inside. The whole building had been painted white so from a distance—a considerable distance—it might look as though it were marble.

His favorite priest, Dolpius, approached him with a familiar smile. He was lanky and tall, with long, greasy hair. Gaius often thought that he must skip meals, since he could see the bones right below the dark skin of each limb. "If only all in Caesar's domain were as pious, then we might have eternal summer at home and ceaseless victory on the frontier." He took Gaius' hand and clasped it in his own. "Are you here to make a sacrifice?"

“Do I come for many other reasons, _sacerdos meus_?”

The priest put his other hand on Gaius’ shoulder. "I hope one day you might come to visit me and not just the gods."

“Not today, _amice_. Today I must make him a generous offer. I have new orders, and with them an opportunity to impress Caesar. I'll be leaving for a while, Dolpius. You must take care.”

Dolpius stumbled over to a cage with a golden fire gecko inside. “We've been saving this one for something special. Just forty denarii and Jupiter himself will want to garner your favor.” He thrust the gecko cage into Gaius’ hands. “And I always take care of myself; it is you that needs the worry.”

Gaius examined the reptile. He eyed it closely for defects or bad omens. After a few moments, he pushed it back to Dolpius. “This one's good. Here.” He reached into his _sacculo_ and pulled out forty of Caesar’s coins. “The prayer is for my assignment,” he paused for a moment, then slowly added, “and for Demetrius.”

Dolpius nodded passively while counting the money. “I will do my job, and the rest is up to the gods. Thank you, Gai.”

Gaius turned his body toward the entrance. "Will you stay for a reading? I may be able to give you insight into this mission of yours."

Gaius shook his head. "No, I think not. My fate remains the same whether I have been informed of it or not." He gave his friend a deep hug. "Farewell. I will be sent directly to the dam afterwards, so it may be some time before we see each other again."

"And I will look to that day with fond longing. And don't worry, your little gecko—he will be gutted in no time."

They separated, and Gaius turned to the bright light of midday outside. He left the massive complex and took the steps back down into the forum. It was even more alive today than it had been recently. The day was good; soon he'd be on a march, and eventually in Flagstaff, where he kept many of his best memories.

After a moment to drink in the surroundings, he pushed off down the crowded, noisy streets to the large urban _domus_ of Balbus.

---

The light brown New Mexican dust of the old parts of Grants gave way to the dark, crushed lava rock that formed the foundation of most of the outer city. Balbus lived far away from the plebeian rot that swarmed the forum and main districts. It wasn't hard to spot the oversized mansion futilely hidden among boarded-up adobe hovels and rusted-out metal shacks.

He first noticed the maintained adobe walls and flourishing front garden. The large metal door stared down at him. Hung from each side were enormous spherical door knockers, one of Sol and another of Luna. He took Sol's orb and clattered it against the dull, hard surface. He heard the clanking as a cacophony, and there was no doubt everybody in the house heard as well.

Nearly 30 seconds passed, and finally, there was the fiddling of locks on the other side. The doors slowly creaked open, revealing a short, sour man who had to be at least 70. The man just looked at him without saying anything, so Gaius began.

“My name is Gaius Octavian Marcinus. I've been sent to the service and command of Senator Rutilius Balbus Equine. Is he home so I may present myself?”

The old man responded in a soft and whispery voice, avoiding eye contact. “Yes, wait here until the master receives you.” The slave shut the oversized doors and left him outside under the pastel sky and above the dark rocky footpath.

He paced back and forth, his blood slowly beginning to boil. How dare that slave? Slamming the door in his face, refusing to look at him, and lacking the proper respect for a legionary. He should be whipped and starved. Slaves were all a cowardly, pathetic lot; if they weren't put to use, then they'd be useless.

He pouted for what seemed like an hour before the metal doors again swung on their hinges and out popped the fattest man Gaius had ever seen. He was more the size of a small car than a man. He could hardly believe those two bloated feet could support the gigantic mass resting atop them. His face had three chins and was completely bald, and when he walked, he had a particular gait—it was more like a wobble than anything else.

When he saw the young legionary, his eyes lit up, and the fat on his face jiggled wildly.

“Caesar told me he'd be sending one of his boys, but he never mentioned it'd be such a pretty one!”

The stench of perfumes and oils finally caught up, and Gaius verged on puking. This was the man he was sent to guard? Were all senators so unworthy?

Balbus continued, “Do come inside. I know you Legion boys don't drink wine, but would you perhaps like some water or lemonade?”

“Water’s fine, thank you, Senator,” Gaius said, reluctantly following his charge inside. The doors once more swung shut with Balbus’ blustering voice still rattling within. His head started to feel tight, and he could tell this assignment was going to be challenging.
